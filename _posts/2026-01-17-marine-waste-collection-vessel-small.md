## 1. Introduction

What if a small collection vessel could treat the plastic that remains after sorting, right on board?

This AZAD Research Group concept places three AFC/SSOF chambers on a small marine waste collection vessel. The layout shows how recovered material would move from the stern collection system to the deck conveyor, which would feed the plastic left after sorting to the three chambers.

A shipboard trial would measure how moisture affects onboard treatment and compare emissions with those from a land-based waste-to-energy plant treating comparable plastic waste.

## 4. Onboard Preparation and Treatment

The small-vessel concept includes a shredder, a dewatering unit, a mechanical agitator, and an automatic ash discharge system.

After sorting, the remaining plastic is shredded and dewatered before it enters the AFC/SSOF chambers. The onboard design also includes a mechanical agitator and an automatic ash discharge system.

A shipboard trial would assess how much dewatering is needed for different loads of recovered plastic and measure emissions during onboard operation.

This area also requires data collection on the results of field tests to improve its structure and performance.

## 5. Chamber controls

Control of the operation of each chamber is possible through the control box on the deck 
and the control system installed in the cabin, allowing for independent or mixed operation.
